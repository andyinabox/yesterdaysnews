I'm still on 5C at the hospital. Last night I had troubling dreams -- first something about vampires, chasing and being chased, the second that Trump was elected president. I woke up to find the second one to be true.

Worst of all, I didn't vote. It was logistically difficult to get a pass to vote, but I could have made it happen. I gave up. Why did I give up? Because it was so easy to stay here, I was so comfortable. I had a book to read. I preferred the escape from reality. 

For this reason I have decided to leave this place, it has become too comfortable. 

Last night we watched the election results com in in the large TV room. I decided to turn in early so that I could save myself the anxiety. I was feeling optimistic before-hand, when my lover came for a visit. But watching the results come in I became less certain.

This morning I went out for fresh air with Will and Nurse Maria. Maria maintained her sunny, smiling face, and Will maintained his cynicism. I was somewhere in-between. We got outside and it had just started drizzling.

This place has lost its novelty, its interest. Well, not completely ... but things that once stood in stark contrast have faded into the background. I have become accustomed.

Last night I woke up to an awful fantasy -- a paranoid daydream. That we truly are lab rats, guinea pigs. That I might not get away so easily. That my doctor is slowly capturing me, the venus fly trap is closing. A delicate system of currents, bringing my healthy body here to become a chemical testing ground.

The fear has mostly dissipated now, or been replaced with the news from yesterday. President Trump. I had an apocalyptic dream; the sky buzzing with warplanes and helicopters, exploding and crashing to the earth. Dark days ahead.

Like a lightning-struck cottonwood, I have fallen. Last night some friends visited. It was good to see them, and yet in seeing them I felt ashamed. I also talked to Brian last night, which was more comforting. He's been fighting a similar battle. We are brothers in this battle against the depths.

I've grown more irritable here at 5 south. Maybe it's a way of shielding myself, lest I want to stay longer because of the people here.

We took a trip to Starbuck's today. This is a ritual reserved for the lifers here, a sacred one. Out through the airlock gate, into the elevator, up one floor, left down the hall, across the great skybridge, through the atrium, past the guard at the sliding door, across the street perilous from speeding cyclists, into our destination; the Barns & Noble / Starbuck's.

This was the second time I participated in this journey (the first was yesterday) -- I tried unsuccessfully earlier but was lacking the required Level 3 clearance. We sat outside despite the cold. I bought nothing because I have no money here, I came for the experience and the camaraderie.

As I write this I check the wall clock, counting the time until lunch (40 minutes). Much of my time here has been spent waiting, especially for meals.

I keep wanting to write about the people here, but have yet to do so. I tell myself it's because of patient confidentiality, but it's really a kind of laziness. (It occurred to me that patient confidentiality would make a nice pun.) Will I get it together?

Today I am feeling no real need to do anything at all. In the time spent here I have desperately been trying to fill the time, avoiding the empty spaces. Mostly reading to fill the gaps. Now it seems unnecessary.

My friend gave me a copy of When Things Fall Apart, written by a Buddhist nun. It is shifting my frame of mind.

So I lied ... shortly after writing the above entry I went off to the library to continue reading Cloud Atlas. Might as well give writing another go.

Last weekend I went on an overnight pass away from NYSPI. My partner picked me up at 3pm, and we went straight to the Trump protests near his eponymous tower. I was excited at first, but once we started marching I got self-conscious with all of the cameras pointed my way. I began to feel that this was all a bit of pageantry, pointless. I got anxious to get going and meet up with my parents at their hotel but my partner held on longer.

At the hotel we found my parents at the bar. TV screens around us were showing images from Trump protests around the nation, and of course I was glad to have been there. We hoofed it over to Armano's -- a usual family-friendly spot in Brooklyn Heights, and had a nice but uneventful dinner. Walked the parents back to the hotel and then we headed home.
